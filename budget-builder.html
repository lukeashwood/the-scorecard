    How this works

    Every starting figure is the government's own, from the current Budget on the Budget page (Budget Paper No. 1, accrual revenue and expenses by category). The bottom line here is revenue minus expenses, which is why it differs from the "underlying cash balance" the Budget headlines. Percentages are shares of the nominal GDP the Budget itself uses.

    This is budget arithmetic, not an economic model. Real changes have knock-on effects it doesn't try to predict: a tax cut can change how much people work and spend, extra spending can add to inflation, and cuts or rises take years to legislate and phase in. Debt and interest effects are simple estimates: extra deficit adds to debt one-for-one, and interest uses the average rate implied by the Budget's own interest bill and debt. Use it to see the trade-offs and the scale of the numbers, not as a forecast.

    Starting figures: Australian Government Budget Paper No. 1, as shown on the Budget page. Everything else is calculated in your browser from those figures; nothing you enter is sent anywhere.
