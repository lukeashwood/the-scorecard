Don't take our word for it

Every number, every source.

We don't make up numbers. Every figure on this site comes from an official publication you can open yourself, and our systems re-check the data every day.

How the data is verified

Most figures come straight from the machine-readable data services of the Australian Bureau of Statistics and the Reserve Bank of Australia. Nobody types them in. Every day an automated process:

  1. Downloads the latest official data files directly from the ABS and RBA.
  2. Recalculates every figure, benchmark and rating from that raw data.
  3. Cross-checks key numbers against a second independent official source. For example, RBA and ABS inflation must agree, and the unemployment rate must match in both. If they don't agree, the figure isn't published.
  4. Range-checks every value, so a corrupted download can't produce an impossible number.
  5. Checks freshness, flagging any source that has stopped updating.
  6. Tests every source link cited on the site.

If a check fails, the site keeps showing the last verified figure with a clear "under review" notice. It never shows a number it couldn't confirm.

Some figures, such as Budget papers, energy regulator decisions and public service headcounts, aren't published as data feeds. These are marked Hand-verified and show the date they were checked against the original document. Each one has a re-check date tied to the source's next release, and the daily process flags any that are overdue.

How ratings work

Each measure is rated On track, Watch or Off track against a benchmark stated on the card: the government's own target or promise, the Reserve Bank's target, the long-run average, or where things stood when the government took office in May 2022. The exact rule for each rating is published under every measure. Measures where more or less isn't clearly better are marked Context and not rated. We also show results that reflect well on the government.

Report an error

If you believe a figure is wrong, tell us which measure and what you think is wrong. It will be checked against the official source and any error corrected promptly.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the numbers come from?

From official publications: mainly the Australian Bureau of Statistics and the Reserve Bank of Australia, plus Treasury's Budget papers and other government agencies for figures that aren't published as data feeds. Every card lists its sources under "Where does this data come from?", and every source is listed further down this page.

How often is the data updated?

An automated process runs every day in the early afternoon, Sydney time, after the ABS's 11:30am release window. It downloads the latest official files, recalculates every figure and rating, and runs the checks listed above. Each card shows when it was last checked.

A figure only changes when the agency publishes new data, so many figures stay the same for weeks or months at a time.

How is each measure rated?

Against a benchmark stated on the card: a government target or promise, the Reserve Bank's target, a long-run average, or where things stood in May 2022. The exact rule is published under each measure, under "How the rating is decided". Measures where more or less isn't clearly better are marked Context and not rated.

What does “Hand-verified” mean?

Some figures, such as Budget papers, energy price determinations and public service headcounts, aren't released as machine-readable data. They are checked by a person against the original document and show the date that happened. Each has a re-check date tied to the source's next release, and the daily process flags any that are overdue.

What happens if a check fails?

The site keeps showing the last verified figure with an "under review" notice. It never publishes a number it couldn't confirm. The full result of the latest run is in the automated check log below.

Does this site use cookies or track me?

No. There are no advertising or analytics cookies and no tracking scripts. Display choices, such as light or dark theme and pinned categories, are saved only in your own browser. You can delete them at any time on the Subscribe page.

When you send a form (an email sign-up, a suggestion or an error report), what you type is sent to the site's team by email through Formspree, a form-handling service. It's used only to send the updates you asked for and to reply to you.

Links from this site to other websites carry a short tag (utm_source=the-scorecard) so those publishers can see visits came from here. It identifies this site, not you.

I think a figure is wrong. What should I do?

Use the report an error form: tell us which measure is wrong and the source you're relying on. It will be checked against the official source and any error corrected promptly. Every card also has a “Report an error” link. You can also suggest a new measure.

Can I reuse these figures?

ABS and RBA data are published under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, so you can reuse them with attribution to the original agency. Use the "Copy citation" button on any card for a ready-made reference that names the source and links back to it.
